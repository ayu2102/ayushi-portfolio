Principles I don’t compromise on

The rules I return to when complexity, constraints, and competing priorities demand a clearer decision.

Don’t break what already works.

Existing behavior carries trust. Improve carefully before replacing familiar patterns.

Reduce cognitive load.

Users should spend energy on their goal, not on understanding the interface.

Build systems, not screens.

A good screen solves one moment. A good system keeps the product coherent as it grows.

Reduce, then refine.

Remove unnecessary complexity before polishing the details that remain.

Workspace · 01 · Current

WordSynk

Enterprise Language Operations Platform

Role
Product Designer
Year
2025 — Present
Type
Enterprise SaaS / Web Platform
Status
In Progress

Modernizing an enterprise language services platform that supports translation, transcription, and interpreting workflows across governments, enterprise organizations, professional linguists, and internal operations teams.

01

The Product

WordSynk powers language services for governments, enterprise organizations, and thousands of professional linguists worldwide.

The platform supports the complete lifecycle of translation, transcription, and interpreting, from customer bookings to assignment management and operational coordination.

Its users range from public-sector organizations placing urgent interpreter requests to linguists accepting work on the move, making reliability and clarity essential to every workflow.

02

The Challenge

The platform had evolved over many years without a unified design direction.

While operationally successful, increasing feature complexity created friction across navigation, accessibility, and consistency, especially for users working under time pressure.

Accessibility

Critical workflows required clearer navigation and more accessible interaction patterns.

Visual Consistency

Patterns had evolved independently across products, creating inconsistent experiences across portals.

Multiple User Groups

Customers, linguists, and internal operations teams approached the platform with different goals, mental models, and levels of urgency.

03

Understanding the Ecosystem

The biggest design challenge was not aesthetics. It was designing a platform that could serve three distinct user groups without creating confusion between them.

Enterprise Clients

Organizations booking interpreting, translation, or transcription services. They need clarity, confidence, and speed, often while making requests under time pressure.

Professional Linguists

Interpreters and translators accepting, managing, and completing assignments. They need a clear, fast, mobile-friendly experience to review and act on work.

Internal Operations Team

Bigword's internal staff managing bookings, assignments, and delivery behind the scenes. They need dense operational information without unnecessary friction.

04

Discovery

Understanding before designing

Before jumping into solutions, discovery focused on understanding where the platform was creating friction and why.

Heuristic Evaluation Feedback Review Stakeholder Conversations Backlog Review

Heuristic Evaluation

Reviewed existing screens against usability principles to identify structural issues such as unclear navigation, weak hierarchy, and missing feedback states.

Feedback Review

Analysed existing user feedback and complaints to identify recurring patterns in what frustrated users most.

Stakeholder Conversations

Worked with Product Owners to understand business constraints, what must not break, and what success should look like for the company.

Backlog Review

Reviewed existing product requests and user stories to understand what pain points had already been captured by other teams.

05

Design Leadership

My role extended beyond interface redesign. I worked across discovery, experience design, stakeholder alignment, and design system consistency.

Discovery

Audited the existing experience and identified usability opportunities across key workflows.

Experience Design

Owned information architecture, user flows, wireframes, and high-fidelity interfaces for redesigned experiences.

Stakeholder Alignment

Presented design rationale, facilitated reviews, and iterated with Product Owners based on business and technical constraints.

Design System

Extended existing components while maintaining consistency across the broader WordSynk platform.

06

What Guided Every Decision

Every design decision had to respect the reality of enterprise systems: people depend on these workflows daily, and even small changes can affect operational continuity.

Clarity over cleverness Accessibility first Serve each user type distinctly Do not break what already works Support business continuity Reduce operational friction

07

Visual Evidence

08

Current Status

The redesign is currently in progress in collaboration with Product Owners and engineering teams.

The customer-facing experience is the current focus, with additional platform areas following as part of the broader modernization effort.

09

Reflection

Designing for enterprise is not about making interfaces simpler for the sake of simplicity.

It is about making complexity manageable for people whose work depends on making the right decision quickly.

Working on WordSynk reinforced that successful enterprise products balance usability, operational constraints, accessibility, and business continuity, not by removing complexity, but by organizing it.

Path2Study

USA Education Funding Platform

Role
UI/UX Designer
Year
2024
Type
Web Application + Admin Portal
Status
Delivered

Designing a USA-focused education funding platform that helps students discover scholarships, explore education loan options, and receive guidance while enabling internal teams to manage scholarships, banking partners, and platform operations.

01

The Opportunity

Students planning to study in the USA often struggle to navigate fragmented scholarship and education loan information spread across multiple websites, banks, and institutions.

Path2Study aimed to become a single destination where prospective students could discover USA scholarships, compare education financing options, and receive personalized guidance.

The platform also included an internal admin portal so the business team could manage scholarships, banks, universities, banners, and operational content.

02

The Challenge

Students had to compare scholarships across multiple sources while also researching loan providers separately.

The lack of centralized information increased decision fatigue and reduced confidence during one of the most important decisions in their education journey.

The challenge was to create a platform that made USA education funding easier to discover, understand, and act on.

03

Product Strategy

Rather than positioning Path2Study as another scholarship directory, the platform was designed as a decision-support system.

The experience combined discovery, personalization, funding information, and guidance within a single product journey.

USA-focused scholarship discovery Education loan information Student profile creation Personalized scholarship recommendations Application tracking 1:1 guidance booking Internal content management Operations management

04

What I Led

I led the design side of the project across the student-facing experience and the internal admin portal.

Information Architecture Website Experience Student Profile Flows Scholarship Discovery Loan Discovery Admin Dashboard Stakeholder Discussions Engineering Collaboration Design Reviews Developer Handoff

05

Product Experience

The product experience was designed across two connected sides: the student-facing platform and the internal administrative portal.

Student Platform

Scholarship discovery, loan exploration, personalized recommendations, application tracking, profile management, and 1:1 booking support.

Admin Portal

Scholarship management, university management, bank and loan partner management, banner content management, user management, and session availability management.

Connected Journey

The student-facing product and admin portal were designed as connected parts of one operating system, not separate one-off surfaces.

06

Visual Evidence

Available visuals are used here to show Path2Study as a complete product experience, not just a set of isolated screens.

07

Leading Beyond Design

Path2Study was my first opportunity to lead a product initiative beyond interface design.

Working closely with business analysts, engineers, and stakeholders taught me how product decisions evolve through discussion, negotiation, and technical constraints, not just visual exploration.

The project strengthened my ability to advocate for users while balancing business priorities, client requirements, and delivery realities.

08

Reflection

This project taught me that product design is not only about creating polished interfaces.

It is also about structuring decisions, coordinating with teams, and helping a product move from idea to delivery despite changing requirements.

Path2Study shaped how I think about design ownership, stakeholder communication, and building products that support both users and the teams operating behind the scenes.

Workspace · 03 · 2022

FactSeal

Background Verification Platform

Role
Associate UI/UX Designer
Year
2022
Type
Mobile App + Internal Web Portal
Status
Completed

FactSeal began as a lightweight app focused mainly on location verification. The redesign expanded it into a complete background screening experience for companies, candidates, and individuals who needed a structured way to verify identity, education, employment, references, documents, consent, and location.

01

Context

FactSeal supports background verification during hiring. Companies can initiate checks for candidates, candidates can submit required information and documents, and individuals can also use the app for personal verification needs.

The product existed before the redesign, but its functionality was limited. It primarily handled current location verification. The redesign expanded both the product scope and the user experience.

02

Problem

The existing app solved only one part of the verification journey.

Background screening involves multiple sensitive steps: identity, education, employment history, certificates, references, consent, document upload, and location verification. Without a clear end-to-end flow, users could easily feel lost, unsure, or overwhelmed.

03

My Role

I redesigned the complete product experience.

Mobile App Internal Web Portal User Flows Information Architecture UI Design Logo Design App Store Assets Play Store Assets

04

Application Flow

  1. 01Onboarding
  2. 02Identity Verification
  3. 03Consent Authorization
  4. 04Form Completion
  5. 05Document Upload
  6. 06Verification Status

The flow was designed to make a complex verification process feel guided, predictable, and easier to complete.

05

Design Decisions

Guided Verification Flow

Instead of exposing users to the entire verification system at once, the experience guides them step by step through the required checks.

Clear Verification States

Each stage communicates what has been completed, what requires attention, and what the user needs to do next.

Sensitive Data, Clear Interaction

Because verification involves personal and professional information, the interface needed to feel structured, transparent, and reliable.

06

Redesigned Experience

The redesigned product included onboarding, OTP verification, consent authorization, identity checks, academic and employment verification flows, location confirmation, document upload, profile screens, and completion states.

  • The internal web portal was redesigned to help teams manage and review verification activity connected to the candidate-facing app.
  • Product screenshots were designed for App Store and Play Store listings across mobile and tablet formats.

07

Looking Back

This project taught me that enterprise product design is not just about adding more functionality. It is about organizing complex workflows into experiences that feel predictable, transparent, and easy to complete.

Product teardown

Windows 11

Observation

Windows 11 carries decades of user familiarity, but familiar systems can still create friction when old interaction patterns and newer behaviours sit side by side.

Insight

Users do not resist change itself. They resist losing predictability. When navigation, settings, and task entry points shift without enough contextual support, even small changes can feel heavier than they are.

Opportunity

Improve feature discovery through contextual entry points, clearer system feedback, and progressive guidance that respects existing user habits instead of forcing users to relearn familiar workflows.

Product teardown

IRCTC

Observation

The product serves a high-pressure need, but users must decode availability, waitlist logic, timing, and payment risk while moving quickly.

Insight

When the stakes are practical and immediate, design has to make system truth visible. Ambiguity damages trust faster than visual roughness.

Opportunity

Use clearer sequencing, stronger status language, and visible recovery routes so people know where they are and what can still be done.

Product teardown

Google Pay

Observation

The payment flow is simple, but the surrounding experience includes offers, rewards, finance prompts, and commerce features competing for attention.

Insight

In money movement, focus is a trust signal. People should never wonder whether the product's priorities are aligned with their task.

Opportunity

Keep the primary payment path visually and structurally protected, then introduce secondary actions only when they are contextual and easy to dismiss.
